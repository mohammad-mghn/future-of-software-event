# TASK 1 — The headlines

## H1. "AI writes more than a quarter of Google's code" (and later 30%, 50%, 75%)

- **Primary.** Sundar Pichai, Q3 2024 earnings remarks, 29 Oct 2024 — https://blog.google/inside-google/message-ceo/alphabet-earnings-q3-2024/ — OPENED-PRIMARY (re-verified; earlier notes had this as snippet-only).
  - Quote: "more than a quarter of all new code … generated by AI, then reviewed and accepted by engineers".
- **What was measured, on whom.** Share of *new* code at Google that is AI-generated and then accepted by an engineer. No unit (lines? characters? accepted autocomplete?), no method, no audit. Population: Google's own engineers. Said on an earnings call, i.e. to investors.
- **What the headline left out.**
  - "New" and "reviewed and accepted by engineers" were dropped; many posts read it as "a quarter of Google's code" or "a quarter of engineers' work".
  - The CEO himself treats the unit loosely. Lex Fridman Podcast #471 (June 2025), transcript ~01:31:16 — https://lexfridman.com/sundar-pichai-transcript/ — OPENED-PRIMARY (human-made transcript): "30% of code now uses AI- generated suggestions or whatever it is".
  - Same passage, the metric he calls more important: "a 10% engineering velocity increase using AI" (~01:31:51). So ~30% of code ≈ ~10% faster engineering, by Google's own estimate.
  - Nothing was said about cutting engineers.
- **Who benefits.** Alphabet sells Gemini, Cloud and coding tools; an earnings call wants an AI-efficiency story. Media: a big round number with "Google" in it.
- **What happened afterwards.**
  - CFO, Q3 2025 call: "nearly half" of code AI-generated (prior notes, OPENED-PRIMARY).
  - 22 Apr 2026, Pichai: "75% of all new code at Google is now AI-generated and approved by engineers" (up from 50% last fall) — https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/google-cloud/cloud-next-2026-sundar-pichai/ — OPENED-PRIMARY (re-verified).
  - Q3 2026 call (28 Oct 2026): no percentage given; claimed 35% more code shipped with 30% fewer errors in recent weeks with a new model (prior notes, OPENED-PRIMARY; vendor claim, no method).
  - Headcount (all Alphabet roles, not just engineers): 181,269 (30 Sep 2024) → 190,167 (30 Sep 2025) — VIA-SECONDARY (release table quoted by Zacks and others) → **204,757 (30 Sep 2026)** — Alphabet 10-Q Q3 2026, https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/GOOG-10-Q-Q3-2026.pdf — OPENED-PRIMARY ("As of September 30, 2026, we had 204,757 employees.").
  - June 2025, Bloomberg Tech: Pichai expected engineering headcount to keep growing into 2026 — https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-06-05/alphabet-ceo-expects-to-keep-hiring-engineers-while-ai-advances — VIA-SECONDARY (paywall; wording from coverage).
  - Caveat for the stage: total headcount rising does not show junior hiring rose; much of the growth may be AI/infrastructure roles. Do not say "AI created jobs at Google".
- **As an opener.** Clarity 5, surprise 3, teaches 4 (unit, speaker, "code ≠ work"). Every number is primary and dated. The cleanest low-risk opener; less dramatic than H3. Stage pair: "25% of new code" → "10% faster" → headcount 181k → 205k.

## H2. "Amodei: AI will write 90% of code in 3–6 months, essentially all in 12"

- **Primary.** Council on Foreign Relations, CEO Speaker Series, 10 Mar 2025, uncorrected transcript — https://www.cfr.org/event/ceo-speaker-series-dario-amodei-anthropic — OPENED-PRIMARY.
  - "in three to six months—where AI is writing 90 percent of the code"
  - "we may be in a world where AI is writing essentially all of the code"
  - Qualifier in the next breath: "the programmer still needs to specify … the conditions of what you're doing"; (paraphrase) humans still decide overall design, how pieces fit, and whether it is secure, so productivity rises while those "islands" remain.
  - The genuinely alarming line, which headlines rarely carried: "eventually all those little islands will get picked off by AI systems".
- **What was measured.** Nothing; it is a forecast. "Code" undefined (lines merged? all code written anywhere?). Scope ambiguous (Anthropic or the industry).
- **What the headline left out.** "May" on the 12-month line; the human-role qualifier; that "90% of code" is not "90% of programmers". Fortune-style paraphrases turned it into "AI will replace all developers within a year" (prior notes, 06).
- **Who benefits.** Anthropic sells Claude and Claude Code; urgency helps sales and fundraising. COI: this note was written by Claude.
- **What happened afterwards.**
  - Redwood Research, 22 Oct 2025: about 50% of merged lines at Anthropic by their estimate, ~90% on some teams; "did not come true as stated" (prior notes, OPENED-PRIMARY).
  - Dreamforce, ~15 Oct 2025, on stage with Benioff: "you need just as many software engineers. You might need more" — Business Insider via AOL, 16 Oct 2025, https://www.aol.com/articles/anthropic-ceo-says-90-code-030401205.html — VIA-SECONDARY (no video or transcript checked).
  - Jan 2026: an Anthropic spokesperson put the company-wide share at 70–90%; Claude Code lead Boris Cherny said his own is 100% — Fortune, 29 Jan 2026, https://fortune.com/2026/01/29/100-percent-of-code-at-anthropic-and-openai-is-now-ai-written-boris-cherny-roon/ — VIA-SECONDARY.
  - May 2026: Anthropic says more than 80% of merged code is Claude-authored; Nov 2026 IPO fact sheet says more than 90% of "contributions" (prior notes, OPENED-PRIMARY; self-reported, unit changes between the two).
  - Anthropic job board, 25 Nov 2026: 636 open ads; 64 product/infra software-engineer titles, 58 of them Staff/Senior/Principal; only 3 early-career-style ads (prior notes 10_live_job_boards, OPENED-PRIMARY via public API). Honest reading: engineers still hired, beginners barely.
- **Verdict.** For code at AI-heavy firms: roughly true, about a year late. "Essentially all" in 12 months: missed. "Fewer engineers": he did not claim it, and it did not happen at his own company; the entry-level door is the open question.
- **As an opener.** Recognition 5, surprise 3, teaches 4 (full quote, deadline check, code vs jobs). Minus: COI for a Claude-compiled note, and "it came partly true" makes a clean debunk dishonest. Better used in the checklist section (Q1, Q6).

## H3. "Zuckerberg: AI will replace mid-level engineers at Meta in 2025" → Meta's 2026 "Project OT"

- **Primary.** The Joe Rogan Experience #2255, released ~10 Jan 2025. Transcript (Happy Scribe) COULD-NOT-OPEN (403). Quotes VIA-SECONDARY: Futurism, 13 Jan 2025, quoting Business Insider — https://futurism.com/the-byte/zuckerberg-automate-coding-ai
  - "Probably in 2025, we at Meta … are going to have an AI that can effectively be a sort of midlevel engineer"
  - "In the beginning it'll be really expensive to run"
  - (paraphrase) Over time much of the code in Meta's apps would be built by "AI engineers instead of people engineers".
- **What was measured.** Nothing; a capability forecast hedged with "probably" and "sort of". Not a layoff plan.
- **What the headline left out/distorted.** "Have an AI that can be a sort of mid-level engineer" became "replace mid-level engineers". The cost caveat vanished.
- **Who benefits.** Meta: an AI-leader story to investors and recruits during record AI capex. Rogan: reach. Media: the verb "replace".
- **What happened afterwards (the strong part).**
  - Jan 2026: leadership retreat set up "Project OT" (Organization Transformation) to make Meta "AI native"; scenarios explored shrinking many teams by up to 60%, in two waves, May and November. Source: Reuters investigation, 26 Aug 2026 (https://www.reuters.com/investigations/mark-zuckerberg-had-bold-plan-replace-meta-staff-with-ai-heres-how-it-imploded-2026-08-26/) — COULD-NOT-OPEN; read via syndication on BNN Bloomberg (https://bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/08/26/mark-zuckerberg-had-a-bold-plan-to-replace-meta-staff-with-ai-heres-how-it-imploded/), Engadget 26 Aug 2026 (https://engadget.com/2244816/meta-reportedly-abandoned-an-ai-focused-restructuring-plan/) and Pragmatic Engineer 3 Sep 2026 (https://blog.pragmaticengineer.com/the-pulse-meta-wanted-to-reduce-teams-by-60-because-of-ai/) — VIA-SECONDARY.
  - 23 Apr 2026 memo: about 10% (~8,000) cut and ~6,000 open roles closed, effective 20 May 2026 — VIA-SECONDARY (NPR/Bloomberg coverage; NPR fetch failed). Fortune, 21 May 2026 (OPENED, secondary): Zuckerberg memo called AI "the most consequential technology of our lifetimes", said "success isn't a given", and expected no further company-wide layoffs in 2026. ~7,000 moved into AI-focused roles.
  - What Reuters reports from internal data (VIA-SECONDARY): code changes **+220%** y/y but new/improved features reaching users only **+36%**; major technical and security incidents **+40%**; time spent firefighting **+70%**; employee favourability **74% → 55%**.
  - November wave cancelled. In July, Zuckerberg reportedly conceded the timing was miscalculated and that agents had not "accelerated" as fast as expected (paraphrase around one quoted word). Meta says the 60% scenarios were not a plan to cut 60% of the workforce.
  - Pragmatic Engineer's own sources: some teams lost 30–40% of engineers; others lost 20–30% to reassignment into AI data labelling (VIA-SECONDARY, anonymous sources).
  - Unverified (search summary only, not opened): 26 employees sued alleging AI tools targeted people on medical leave. Do not use on stage.
- **Verdict.** The 2025 forecast missed. In 2026 the company acted on the belief anyway; output rose far more than outcome; the AI underdelivered; 8,000 people were still laid off.
- **As an opener.** Surprise 5, story arc 5, teaches 5: forecast vs measurement; "code" vs "features users get"; and the uncomfortable truth that **what executives believe can move jobs even when the technology does not deliver**. Risks: Reuters original not opened, Rogan quote via secondary. Before the event the host should read the Reuters piece and find the clip timestamp. Handle the 8,000 people with respect; this is not a "haha, AI failed" story.

## H4. "Salesforce won't hire any software engineers in 2025 because of AI"

- **Primary.**
  - 20VC with Harry Stebbings, reported 18 Dec 2024 — https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-will-hire-no-more-software-engineers-in-2025-says-marc-benioff/ — VIA-SECONDARY: "We're not adding any more software engineers next year", tied to a productivity gain of "more than 30%". Same interview (paraphrase): fewer support engineers; another 1,000–2,000 salespeople; Salesforce probably larger in five years.
  - Q4 FY25 earnings call, 26 Feb 2025 — The Register, 27 Feb 2025, https://www.theregister.com/2025/02/27/salesforce_misses_revenue_guidance/ — VIA-SECONDARY (transcript-based): "we're not going to hire any new engineers this year"; "30 percent productivity increase on engineering".
- **What was measured.** "30% productivity": no definition, no method, measured by the vendor on its own product (Agentforce), told to investors on a call where revenue guidance missed.
- **What the headline left out.** A hiring pause is not a layoff of engineers; backfills continue; the same CEO was adding salespeople; "30%" is undefined.
- **Who benefits.** Salesforce: "we use Agentforce ourselves" is a sales pitch, and a flat engineering line is a margin story.
- **What happened afterwards.**
  - 28 Apr 2025: Salesforce Ben counted 106 openings in the "Engineering Team" filter and 189 for the search "Software Engineer" on Salesforce's careers page — https://www.salesforceben.com/salesforce-is-aggressively-hiring-in-these-areas-what-to-watch-out-for/ — OPENED (secondary check of a primary page; keyword matches are loose).
  - Sept 2025: Benioff said customer support headcount fell from ~9,000 to ~5,000 using AI agents (ITPro coverage of The Logan Bartlett Show) — VIA-SECONDARY.
  - Jan 2026, TBPN: "held my engineering headcount mostly flat"; "about 15,000 engineers"; 20% more account executives — ITPro, 15 Jan 2026, https://www.itpro.com/business/business-strategy/marc-benioff-salesforce-software-engineering-hiring-flat-ai — VIA-SECONDARY.
  - 28 May 2026, Q1 FY27 call: "We're not hiring more engineers"; growth "mostly" in sales — Fortune, https://fortune.com/2026/05/28/ai-slashes-white-collar-jobs-salesforce-ceo-marc-benioff-one-department-still-hiring-sales/ — VIA-SECONDARY.
- **Verdict.** Held, in its softer form: engineering flat at ~15,000 for about two years, not zero hires. The visible cuts were in support, not engineering.
- **As an opener.** Recognition 3, surprise 2, teaches 3 (freeze vs firing; ask "and which roles grew?").

## H5. "Microsoft / Amazon / Block cut jobs because of AI" (one family, three cases)

### H5a. Microsoft, May 2025: "Microsoft lays off engineers as AI writes 30% of its code"
- **Primary pieces.**
  - Nadella at LlamaCon, 29 Apr 2025: "maybe 20, 30 percent of the code" in some projects written by software — VIA-SECONDARY (AP/PBS; CNBC per prior notes).
  - Layoff of ~6,000 (~3%), notices from 13 May 2025. Microsoft statement: "organizational changes necessary to best position the company for success in a dynamic marketplace"; CFO Hood, April call: "reducing layers with fewer managers"; FY25 AI infrastructure spend ~$80B — AP via PBS, https://www.pbs.org/newshour/economy/microsoft-is-laying-off-about-3-of-its-workforce — OPENED (secondary, carries the primary statement).
  - Washington WARN: 1,985 roles (VIA-SECONDARY). Bloomberg: software engineering was the largest single category, over 40% of ~2,000 WA notices; Bloomberg's headline pointed at rising AI *costs* — https://news.bloomberglaw.com/daily-labor-report/microsoft-layoffs-hit-coders-hardest-with-ai-costs-on-the-rise — VIA-SECONDARY (paywall after the lede). WARN filing itself not opened.
- **Distortion.** Two true facts placed side by side (code share; engineers cut) implied a cause Microsoft never stated. The better-supported driver in the reporting is cost pressure from AI capex, not AI doing the engineers' work.
- **Who benefits.** Media: a ready-made causal story. Microsoft: looks AI-efficient to investors without claiming replacement.
- **Afterwards.** ~9,000 more on 2 Jul 2025 (<4%; VIA-SECONDARY). Nadella memo, 24 Jul 2025 — https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/07/24/recommitting-to-our-why-what-and-how/ — OPENED-PRIMARY: "the enigma of success in an industry that has no franchise value"; overall headcount "relatively unchanged". 2026: a reported ~8,750 US buyout plan (May), 4,800 cuts (~2.1%, July), and on 26 Oct 2026 a Voluntary Exit Offer to ~60,000 employees (~27% of ~223,000); AP does not cite AI replacing workers, and places it beside record AI spending — https://www.wtoc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-offers-60000-employees-voluntary-buyouts-ai-spending-accelerates/ — VIA-SECONDARY.

### H5b. Amazon, Jun 2025 – Jan 2026: "Amazon cuts corporate jobs because of AI"
- **Primary pieces.**
  - Jassy memo, 17 Jun 2025 — https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-ceo-andy-jassy-on-generative-ai — OPENED-PRIMARY: "we expect that this will reduce our total corporate workforce" over "the next few years"; he also says it is "hard to know exactly where this nets out".
  - Galetti memo, 28 Oct 2025, ~14,000 corporate roles — https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/company-news/amazon-workforce-reduction — OPENED-PRIMARY: AI is the "most transformative technology we've seen since the Internet"; Amazon must be organised "more leanly, with fewer layers and more ownership".
  - Jassy, Q3 2025 call, 30 Oct 2025: "not really financially driven, and it's not even really AI-driven, not right now at least"; "Really, it's culture." $1.8B severance charge — GeekWire, https://www.geekwire.com/2025/its-culture-amazon-ceo-says-massive-corporate-layoffs-were-about-agility-not-ai-or-cost-cutting/ — VIA-SECONDARY (call transcript not opened).
- **Distortion.** Within four months the company offered three framings (AI will shrink us; AI is transformative so we flatten layers; it is not really AI, it is culture). Headlines picked "AI".
- **Who benefits.** Amazon: "AI" reads as forward-looking to investors, "culture" reads better to employees and regulators. Media: "AI" is the clickable one.
- **Afterwards.** 28 Jan 2026: ~16,000 more corporate cuts; ~30,000 since Oct 2025, "nearly 10%" of corporate staff, largest in company history (2022: ~27,000); framed as "reducing layers, increasing ownership, removing bureaucracy" while profit was rising — AP/Reuters coverage, e.g. https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/1/28/amazon-cuts-thousands-of-jobs-amid-ai-push — VIA-SECONDARY.

### H5c. Block, 26 Feb 2026: "Jack Dorsey's Block lays off nearly half its staff because of AI"
- **Primary.** Q4 2025 shareholder letter — https://s29.q4cdn.com/628966176/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter_Block.pdf — OPENED-PRIMARY (text extracted from the PDF):
  - "reducing Block by nearly half, from over 10,000 people to just under 6,000"
  - "2025 was a strong year for us."
  - "A significantly smaller team, using the tools we're building, can do more"
  - Forecast: "the majority of companies will reach the same conclusion" within a year.
  - Q4 gross profit $2.87B, +24% y/y.
- **What was measured.** Nothing about AI productivity; it is a CEO's thesis. The letter offers no internal productivity data.
- **What the headline left out.** Headcount roughly tripled 2019–2022; a ~$68M company event about five months earlier — Quartz via Yahoo, 27 Feb 2026, https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jack-dorseys-block-lays-off-142627724.html — VIA-SECONDARY. Ethan Mollick (quoted there): "worth taking the justification with a grain of salt".
- **Who benefits (visible in one number).** Shares rose ~23% in late trading after the announcement — The Register, 27 Feb 2026, https://www.theregister.com/2026/02/27/block_q4_2025_ai_layoffs/ — VIA-SECONDARY. "AI" plays better with investors than "we over-hired".
- **Afterwards.** A further round in Cash App/Square operations teams the day after Q3 2026 results, size undisclosed — VIA-SECONDARY (date and size unconfirmed). Whether "most companies" followed by Feb 2027: the clock is running; see Challenger below.

### Cross-check for the whole family
- Challenger, Gray & Christmas, October 2026 report (published 5 Nov 2026) — https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Challenger-Report-October-2026.pdf — OPENED-PRIMARY (re-verified from PDF): AI cited in **121,401** announced US cuts Jan–Oct 2026 (~20%); **1,265** in October, the lowest month of 2026; "Market/Economic Conditions" overtook AI as the top stated reason YTD (130,531). ## H6. "MIT report: 95% of generative AI pilots at companies are failing"

- **Headline.** Fortune, 18 Aug 2025 — https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/ — OPENED (secondary). Report quoted there: "the vast majority stall, delivering little to no measurable impact on P&L".
- **Primary.** "The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025", MIT NANDA (a Media Lab project on agent networks), dated July 2025, labelled preliminary. COULD-NOT-OPEN: the NANDA page (https://www.media.mit.edu/groups/nanda/overview/, OPENED-PRIMARY) routes "REPORTS" through an access form; no direct PDF.
- **Method, as best reconstructed.**
  - Fortune: 150 leader interviews, 350-employee survey, 300 public deployments.
  - Report summaries (AIGL, https://www.aigl.blog/state-of-ai-in-business-2025/, VIA-SECONDARY) and 80,000 Hours: **52** interviews, **153** survey responses, 300+ public initiatives, Jan–Jun 2025. The two descriptions conflict; unresolved at primary.
- **What the 95% really is.** Rob Wiblin, 80,000 Hours episode (recorded 13 Feb 2026, page dated 28 Apr 2026) — https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/ai-workplace-mit-study/ — OPENED (secondary analysis of the report). Paraphrase:
  - Of all organisations surveyed: ~60% investigated custom AI tools, ~20% piloted, ~5% deployed one successfully. So 80% never piloted; calling them "failed pilots" is wrong.
  - Among those that piloted, roughly a quarter met the bar, and the bar was high: a "marked and sustained" productivity or P&L impact within six months.
  - The 5% may rest on two or three companies; uncertainty is very wide.
  - Over 90% of staff used ChatGPT-type tools; the report set this aside as individual, not P&L, productivity.
  - Authors include people building or commercialising agent frameworks; the report recommends NANDA-style agentic frameworks; no conflict-of-interest note. Not peer-reviewed; not public when it went viral. The FT linked it to a Nasdaq sell-off.
- **Buried line relevant to juniors.** Per Fortune, companies were increasingly "not backfilling positions as they become vacant" instead of mass layoffs. That is the entry-door story, and almost no headline carried it.
- **Who benefits.** "AI bubble" narratives and sellers of that narrative; the authors' agent-framework project; MIT's brand did the persuading; Fortune got a mega-viral headline.
- **Afterwards.** Became a standard citation in 2025–26. Better-measured counterpart: McKinsey 2026, 37% of firms report any EBIT effect (prior notes 07, OPENED-PRIMARY). HPCwire critique (https://www.hpcwire.com/bigdatawire/2025/09/04/mit-report-flags-95-genai-failure-rate-but-critics-say-it-oversimplifies/) COULD-NOT-OPEN (403).
- **As an opener.** Surprise 4, teaches 5 (denominator, success definition, "can I open it?", authors' stake). But it is a *comforting* headline, so it fits best as the mid-talk twist: "the lens works on good news too."

## H7. "METR: AI makes experienced developers 19% slower"

- **Primary.** METR, 10 Jul 2025 — https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/ (paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09089) — OPENED-PRIMARY (re-verified).
  - Result: developers "take 19% longer than without—AI makes them slower".
  - Sample: 16 experienced open-source developers, 246 real issues in repositories they had worked on for years; tools mostly Cursor Pro with Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet (Feb–Jun 2025). They forecast 24% faster and afterwards still believed 20% faster.
  - METR's own limit: "We do not claim that our developers or repositories represent a majority" of software work; also not a claim about future tools.
- **What the headline left out.** n=16; expert maintainers on their own huge repos; early-2025 tools; METR now marks it out of date. The durable finding is the **gap between felt (+20%) and measured (−19%)**.
- **Who benefits.** AI-sceptic content. METR is a nonprofit with no product, though it works with labs.
- **Afterwards.** METR update, 24 Feb 2026 — https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/ — OPENED-PRIMARY (re-verified): returning developers −18% (CI −38% to +9%); new recruits −4% (CI −15% to +9%). The post never defines the sign; read with the 2025 "+19% longer", negative means less time, i.e. faster (inference). METR calls it "an unreliable signal": many developers "do not wish to work without AI", and 30–50% withheld tasks, which biases the estimate (paraphrase). Field RCTs with autocomplete-era Copilot found +26% completed tasks (Cui et al., Management Science 2026; prior notes 01).
- **As an opener.** Recognition 3 (dev-Twitter knows it), surprise 3, teaches 4 (sample size, date of tools, felt vs measured). Do not present the 19% as today's speed.

## H8. Klarna: "AI does the work of 700 agents" → "Klarna rehires humans"

- **Primary.** Klarna press release, 27 Feb 2024 — https://www.klarna.com/international/press/klarna-ai-assistant-handles-two-thirds-of-customer-service-chats-in-its-first-month/ — OPENED-PRIMARY: "the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents"; 2.3 million conversations (two-thirds of chats); resolution under 2 minutes vs 11; an estimated $40M profit improvement in 2024. No layoffs stated; customers can still ask for a human.

# TASK 2 — Checklist: questions to ask of any AI-and-jobs headline

Each question comes from a failure above. Short enough for one slide.

1. **What exactly was counted, and is it the thing I care about?**
   Born from: Google's "% of new code … or whatever it is" vs a 10% velocity gain (H1); Meta's +220% code changes vs +36% features shipped (H3); Challenger filing Amazon warehouse hires as "Technology" (H5). Code ≠ work ≠ jobs.
2. **Out of how many, and who was in the sample?**
   Born from: MIT's 95% resting on 52 interviews and 153 responses, where 80% never piloted (H6); METR's 16 developers (H7); NY Fed's one ACS year of 22–27-year-olds (H10).
3. **Can I open the original, and does it say what the headline says?**
   Born from: MIT report behind a form when it went viral (H6); Builder.ai's "700" traced to one crypto enthusiast's tweet (H9); "sort of a mid-level engineer" becoming "replace" (H3); Amodei's qualifier cut (H2).
4. **When was it measured, and is it still true?**
   Born from: 2023 data sold in 2025 as "suddenly" (H10); early-2025 tools quoted as today's speed (H7); Klarna's 2024 claim vs its 2026 explanation (H8).
5. **Who is speaking, to whom, and what do they gain?**
   Born from: earnings calls to investors (H1, H4, H5b); Block's shares +23% the evening it blamed AI (H5c); MIT authors promoting their own agent framework (H6); an AI-lab CEO forecasting AI coding (H2).
6. **Is it a forecast, a plan, or a measurement? If a forecast, has its deadline passed?**
   Born from: "3–6 months" (H2); "probably in 2025" (H3); "in the next few years" (H5b); "the majority of companies … within the next year" (H5c, deadline Feb 2027).
7. **Was the cause shown, or were two facts just placed side by side?**
   Born from: "AI writes 30% of code" + "engineers laid off" (H5a); Amazon's own "not even really AI-driven" (H5b); stated layoff reasons ≠ causes (Challenger).
8. **What happened next, and does the opposite headline fail too?**
   Born from: Klarna's "reversal" that wasn't (H8); Meta's cancelled second wave (H3); METR's own update (H7); "AI is fake" (H9) and "AI fails 95%" (H6) being as wrong as "AI replaces everyone".

Pocket version (three questions for a slide or a phone note): **What was counted? Who is talking? What happened next?**

Habit that goes with Q6: keep a small "prediction ledger" (claim, who, date said, deadline, outcome). Live entries to check: Block "most companies within a year" (Feb 2027); Amodei's Jan 2026 "6–12 months" for most end-to-end SWE work (prior notes 06).

# TASK 3 — Signals to watch yourself, for free

Rule of thumb for the talk: watch 3–4 of these once a month; never react to one number alone; when they disagree, that disagreement is the information.

1. **Software-development job postings index (Indeed Hiring Lab, via FRED)**
   - Where: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/IHLIDXUSTPSOFTDEVE (raw CSV also at https://github.com/hiring-lab/job_postings_tracker).
   - Updates: daily series (7-day), US only; FRED refreshed weekly-ish (obs 13 Nov 2026 posted 17 Nov). History gets revised.
   - Latest (OPENED-PRIMARY, CSV checked): **81.7 on 13 Nov 2026** (Feb 2020 = 100) → +23% y/y (66.2 on 13 Nov 2025), +34% from the low of 61.1 (17 May 2025), still ~18% below Feb 2020 and ~65% below the 233.8 peak (28 Feb 2022). On ChatGPT's launch day (30 Nov 2022) it was 142.5, already falling.
   - Rise: employers advertising more (Hiring Lab says 2025–26 growth was mostly senior and AI-titled; prior notes 02). Fall: fewer ads. Trap: ads ≠ hires; one board; start-date choice changes the story ("−65%" vs "+34%").
2. **Payroll employment, Computer Systems Design & Related Services (BLS, via FRED)**
   - Where: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CES6054150001
   - Updates: monthly, first Friday; revised for two months.
   - Latest (OPENED-PRIMARY, CSV checked): **2,354.1k in Oct 2026** → −1.1% y/y (2,379.7k), −5.2% from the Mar 2023 peak (2,483.5k), +5.4% vs Feb 2020 (2,234.1k).
   - Rise/fall: actual US jobs in IT services firms. Trap: includes non-programmers; excludes developers employed at banks, retailers, etc.
   - Teaching pair with #1: postings +23% y/y while employment −1.1% y/y. Ads move first; jobs lag; neither alone is "the market".
3. **TrueUp open tech jobs**
   - Where: https://www.trueup.io/job-trend and https://www.trueup.io/engineering/reports
   - Updates: daily; covers big tech, unicorns and funded startups (worldwide listings, US-heavy).
   - Latest: 281,328 open tech jobs on 11 Nov 2026, 41.1% below peak, 72.6% above low; 65,351 engineering jobs (prior notes 02, OPENED-PRIMARY). COULD-NOT-OPEN today (403).
   - Rise: the companies juniors dream about are hiring. Trap: sells job-search products; not comparable to Indeed.
4. **Layoff trackers (read together)**
   - layoffs.fyi (https://layoffs.fyi/) — continuous, tech companies worldwide, built from public reports. 2026: 128,536 people at 299 companies by 10 Sep 2026, already above all of 2025 — VIA-SECONDARY (live page is script-rendered; not read).
   - TrueUp layoffs (https://www.trueup.io/layoffs): 196,349 people affected in 2026 as of 23 Oct — VIA-SECONDARY.
   - Challenger monthly report (https://www.challengergray.com/, early each month): AI as a *stated* reason ~20% of US announced cuts YTD, 2.9% in October 2026 (OPENED-PRIMARY).
   - Rise: more announced cuts. Trap: announcements ≠ job losses; all roles, not engineers; stated reasons are PR. Layoffs and postings can rise together (2026 did).
5. **NY Fed, Labor Market for Recent College Graduates**
   - Where: https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market
   - Updates: unemployment/underemployment quarterly (Feb, May, Aug, Nov); by-major table and wages annually in February (next: Feb 2027, with 2025 data).
   - Latest (OPENED-PRIMARY): 2026 Q3, recent grads 5.2% unemployed, 42.5% underemployed. By major (2024 data): CS 7.0% / 19.1% (prior notes).
   - Rise in CS unemployment: the entry door is narrowing. Always read underemployment and the data year alongside it.
6. **Stanford Digital Economy Lab / ADP "Canaries" dashboard**
   - Where: https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/project/canaries-dashboard/
   - Updates: monthly (last 21 Oct 2026) — OPENED-PRIMARY. ~25,000 firms using ADP payroll.
   - Shows employment of early-career workers in AI-exposed occupations vs others (e.g., past year, most-exposed: −5.4% early-career women, −3.1% men). Correlation, not cause.
   - Widening gap = the young-worker squeeze continues; closing gap = it was cyclical.
7. **The entry door itself: careers pages, intern and new-grad lists**
   - Company boards are often public APIs (Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever); count software-engineer ads and how many say intern/new grad/junior. Example (prior notes 10, OPENED-PRIMARY): Anthropic 636 ads on 25 Nov 2026, only 3 early-career-style.
   - Community lists, updated daily: https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions and https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/Summer2026-Internships (now redirects to Summer2027-Internships) — OPENED-PRIMARY (repo pages; listing counts not read). US/Canada-heavy.
   - Iran: count "junior/کارآموز" programming ads on Jobinja, Jobvision, Quera Jobs once a month, same day, same filters; no official Iranian series exists (prior notes 02). In Iran the measured shocks of 1404–05 were war and internet shutdowns (Jobvision tech ads −60% during the 21-day shutdown, prior notes), not AI; connectivity status (e.g. Cloudflare Radar's Iran page, not opened here) is a relevant local signal.
   - Rising count of entry-level ads at the same companies = door reopening.
8. **Hacker News "Ask HN: Who is hiring?" (monthly thread)**
   - Where: https://hnhiring.com/ (index of the threads), thread posted on the first weekday of each month.
   - Counts (OPENED-PRIMARY): Nov 2022: 544; Nov 2025: 399; **Nov 2026: 238 as of 16 Nov** (may still grow).
   - Rise/fall: startup and remote-friendly hiring mood. Trap: tiny, self-selected, HN's audience changes. It currently disagrees with #1 (down vs up), which fits "senior/large-employer recovery, startup and junior weakness" but does not prove it.
9. **Capability: METR quarterly capability updates**
   - Where: https://metr.org/blog/ (latest: https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/). The famous chart page https://metr.org/time-horizons/ says it is "no longer actively updated" (OPENED-PRIMARY).
   - Latest (OPENED-PRIMARY, first part of post read): 50%-success time horizon doubling every ~3.6 months (95% CI 2.08–4.94); top model ~40 hours with a CI of 13 to 2,645 hours; only 9% of human-time estimates come directly from measured humans.
   - Rise: agents finish longer bounded tasks. Trap: METR's own FAQ says an 8-hour horizon does not mean AI can do 8 hours of a high-context professional's work. Capability ≠ jobs.
10. **Capability: coding benchmarks, and whether they are still meaningful**
    - SWE-bench (https://www.swebench.com/) and SWE-bench Pro (https://scale.com/leaderboard/swe_bench_pro_public); update whenever a model ships.
    - OpenAI stopped reporting SWE-bench Verified on 23 Feb 2026, saying it is "increasingly contaminated" and recommending SWE-bench Pro — https://openai.com/index/why-we-no-longer-evaluate-swe-bench-verified/ — OPENED-PRIMARY. SWE-Bench Pro V2 reached 99.4% after repairs (Oct 2026); Cognition's FrontierCode 2.0 "would a maintainer merge it?" top score 47.3% (prior notes 01).
    - Rise on a fresh benchmark = real capability gain on curated tasks. A benchmark near 100% means "find a harder one", not "programmers are finished". Watch the gap between "tests pass" and "a maintainer would merge".

How to read them together (inference, for the host): in Nov 2026 postings (#1) and big-company openings (#3) are up, payroll employment (#2) is slightly down, startup threads (#8) are down, announced layoffs (#4) are up, and the young-worker gap (#5, #6) persists. That pattern says "reshuffling and a narrower entry door", not "collapse" and not "all clear".
